Task 2: Interview Questions & Answers

1. What is phishing?**
Phishing is a type of cyber attack where attackers trick people into sharing sensitive information (like passwords, credit card numbers, or personal data) or installing malware by pretending to be a trustworthy entity, usually via email or text.

2. How to identify a phishing email?**
You can spot a phishing email by checking for:
- Mismatched or suspicious sender email addresses.
- Urgent or threatening language demanding immediate action.
- Generic greetings (like "Dear Customer").
- Poor grammar or spelling mistakes.
- Suspicious links or unexpected attachments.

3. What is email spoofing?**
Email spoofing is a technique where an attacker alters the email header details (specifically the 'From' field) to make the email appear as if it came from a legitimate, trusted source, hiding the actual sender's identity.

4. Why are phishing emails dangerous?**
They are dangerous because they exploit human psychology rather than software flaws. They can lead to severe data breaches, financial loss, identity theft, and corporate network compromises if a user clicks a bad link or downloads a malicious attachment.

5. How can you verify the sender's authenticity?**
- Carefully check the actual domain after the '@' symbol in the sender's email address.
- Inspect the email headers for authentication protocols like SPF (Sender Policy Framework), DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail), and DMARC.
- Contact the sender/organization directly through an official, independent channel to verify.

6. What tools can analyze email headers?**
There are several free online header analysis tools, such as:
- Google Admin Toolbox (Messageheader)
- MXToolbox
- Mailheader.org

7. What actions should be taken on suspected phishing emails?**
- Do NOT click any links or download any attachments.
- Report the email to your organization's IT/Security team or mark it as phishing/spam in your email client.
- Delete the email permanently from your inbox.

8. How do attackers use social engineering in phishing?**
Attackers use social engineering by manipulating human emotions like fear, urgency, curiosity, or greed. For example, they might fake an urgent bank security alert or offer a fake lottery win to bypass a user's natural caution, forcing them to act quickly before checking the facts.
